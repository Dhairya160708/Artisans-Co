We arrive in Bagru before seven, while the light is still soft enough to
photograph without flattening the indigo. Fatima Bano's workshop sits at the
edge of the village, a low courtyard building with a well at its centre and
long wooden printing tables running the length of the main room.

## The blocks

Each block is teak, hand-carved by a family workshop two villages over, and
some of the ones Fatima uses are older than she is. A single motif might
require three or four separate blocks — one for outline, one or two for
fill colour, one for over-dye — each registered by eye against the last.

There is no ruler in sight. The registration is done entirely by hand,
which is why no two lengths of cloth, however similar, are ever quite
identical.

## Drying in the open

By mid-morning the courtyard is lined with freshly printed cotton, laid flat
in the sun to cure. Fatima tells us — through her son, who translates — that
the dye needs direct light to fix properly, which is why nothing here is
printed on a cloudy day.
