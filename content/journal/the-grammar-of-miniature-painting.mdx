Rajendra Sharma's studio is a single room with north-facing light, a low
desk, and shelves of small glass jars — each holding a mineral pigment he
has ground himself. Before any painting begins, the paper itself has to be
prepared.

## Burnishing the wasli

Sheets of handmade wasli paper are pasted together in layers, then
burnished with a smooth agate stone for hours until the surface takes on a
faint sheen. Only then can pigment sit correctly on it without bleeding.

## Pigment and line

The palette comes from stone: malachite for green, lapis for blue, ochre
and cinnabar for the warmer tones. Gum arabic binds them. The finest
outlines are laid with a brush made from a single squirrel hair, sharpened
to a point finer than a pencil lead.

## Gold, last

Gold detailing — jewellery, borders, the edges of a garment — is always
applied last, and always in natural daylight, since artificial light can
make the tone misleading.
